Modern Mammals Amazon Growth Strategy

Prepared by FTR  ·  Amazon & Walmart Growth

Summary

Modern Mammals has built a differentiated product offering and a loyal customer base, but Amazon has primarily operated as a secondary sales channel, capturing demand generated through off-Amazon marketing.

The opportunity is to change that.

Our primary objective is to establish Amazon as an independent customer acquisition channel - reaching shoppers who are not already familiar with Modern Mammals and building sustainable growth through Amazon search, product discovery, and repeat purchases.

The brand's target of $2M in annual Amazon sales will require more than restarting advertising. We need to strengthen the foundations that allow Modern Mammals to compete with established hair-care brands: product positioning, discoverability, competitive offers, customer trust, and retention.

Key Priorities:
  • Expand beyond branded searches into relevant, higher-volume hair-care categories.
  • Optimize product listings to communicate benefits, differentiate the products, and convert unfamiliar shoppers.
  • Strengthen the product catalog with more accessible entry-point and replenishment offers.
  • Build customer trust through reviews, creator content, and stronger product education.
  • Increase repeat purchases through Subscribe & Save and targeted retention campaigns.
  • Scale advertising based on acquisition performance and product-level profitability.
Current Amazon Position
  • Monthly revenue declined from approximately $87K in March to $36.5K in September 2026, alongside a significant reduction in advertising activity and the discontinuation of key products.
  • Sessions declined approximately 80% over the same period. The current high unit-session percentage is influenced by repeat and subscription orders and should not be treated as a baseline for new-customer acquisition.
  • Approximately 98% of purchases reported in Amazon's search data came from branded searches, highlighting limited visibility among shoppers unfamiliar with Modern Mammals.
  • The discontinued 32oz Magic Mud refill previously contributed approximately 38% of April revenue, leaving a gap in the replenishment catalog.
Amazon as a Customer Acquisition Channel

Modern Mammals has historically benefited from customers discovering the brand through external marketing and subsequently purchasing on Amazon.

We should continue capturing that demand efficiently, but it cannot be the primary growth strategy if the objective is to make Amazon an independent channel.

The focus is to move from capturing existing demand to generating new demand directly on Amazon.

This requires expanding beyond niche shampoo-alternative searches and reaching shoppers already looking for solutions in established categories.

Search Opportunity
Magic Mud - Expand into conventional hair-care searches

Priority opportunities include:

  • Men's shampoo and conditioner 2-in-1 - approximately 10.1K monthly searches
  • Co-wash - approximately 6.4K
  • Dry scalp shampoo for men - approximately 791
  • Broader men's shampoo searches - approximately 100.7K

Magic Mud's challenge is convincing conventional shampoo shoppers to consider a different approach to washing their hair.

Light Bar - Compete within an established shampoo-bar category

Priority opportunities include:

  • Shampoo bar - approximately 81.6K monthly searches
  • Bar shampoo - approximately 22.1K
  • Bar shampoo and conditioner - approximately 13.3K
  • Shampoo and conditioner bar - approximately 7.5K

Light Bar has a more straightforward category fit because shoppers are already searching for the product format. However, competition is established, making differentiation and customer trust particularly important.

Competitive Landscape

Magic Mud: Competes against established conventional shampoo brands with familiar products, lower entry prices, and stronger review histories. The current $68 2-pack and limited review base create additional barriers for first-time buyers.

Light Bar: Competes in an established shampoo-bar category where leading products have thousands of reviews and often lower entry prices. Its existing review base is stronger than Magic Mud's, but the brand needs a clearer reason for shoppers to choose it over established alternatives.

Growth Expectations

Building an independent acquisition channel will take time.

Initial non-branded traffic may convert less efficiently than existing branded demand. Higher acquisition costs are possible while the brand builds visibility, review volume, and customer trust.

We recommend a controlled approach: strengthen the listings and offers first, then introduce and scale non-branded advertising based on performance.

Success should be measured through non-branded visibility, new-to-brand customer acquisition, conversion, advertising efficiency, and repeat purchasing - not revenue growth alone.

Growth Area 1 | Catalog & Offer Strategy

Objective: Strengthen the product catalog to lower barriers for first-time buyers, introduce more accessible entry-point offers, and create better replenishment options that support repeat purchases and long-term customer value.

Recommended Actions
Introduce a single-unit Magic Mud offer
  • Evaluate launching the 8oz Magic Mud single at the proposed $38 price point.
  • Position it as the entry offer for shoppers unfamiliar with the brand and hesitant to commit to the $68 2-pack.
  • Use the existing 2-pack as the core value offer and a natural next purchase.
Restore a larger Magic Mud replenishment option
  • Confirm the status and availability of the proposed 4 × 8oz Magic Mud pack.
  • Evaluate launching it on Amazon to replace the replenishment role previously served by the discontinued 32oz refill pouch.
  • Position the larger pack around convenience, value, and longer-term use.

The discontinued refill was a major revenue driver and had an established subscription base. Its removal created a gap in the current product catalog.

Optimize variation structure
  • Review the Magic Mud 2-pack and Magic Mud + Light Bar bundle relationship.
  • Consider separating the bundle into a standalone listing to allow dedicated positioning, SEO, and A+ content.
  • Evaluate the impact on existing discovery and performance before making changes.
  • Maintain the current Light Bar variation family.
Growth Area 2 | Listing Optimization & Product Positioning

Objective: Fully optimize the Magic Mud and Light Bar listings to improve organic search visibility, strengthen product differentiation, increase click-through rates, and convert more shoppers who are unfamiliar with the brand.

Recommended Actions
Complete end-to-end SEO, content, and creative optimization
  • Keyword research and SEO strategy: Complete keyword research, competitor analysis, search intent mapping, and keyword prioritization to identify the strongest organic growth opportunities.
  • Product positioning: Refine the positioning of each product around customer needs, key benefits, competitive differentiation, and the reasons shoppers should choose Modern Mammals.
  • Full listing copy optimization: Develop fully optimized product titles, item highlights, product descriptions, backend search terms, and relevant product attributes, balancing keyword relevance with clear, persuasive messaging.
  • Main image optimization: Develop and test high-impact main images designed to improve visibility and click-through rates in Amazon search results.
  • Complete image stack: Develop supporting images covering product benefits, differentiation, how-to-use instructions, product comparisons, and common customer questions.
  • Premium A+ Content: Develop comprehensive, mobile-optimized Premium A+ content that strengthens product education, brand storytelling, and conversion.
  • AI search optimization: Optimize product attributes, content structure, and product information to improve relevance and discoverability across Amazon's traditional search and AI-powered shopping experiences.
  • Conversion optimization: Ensure the full listing experience is consistent, informative, and designed to address customer objections and support purchasing decisions.
Lead with the customer problem and product benefits

Focus messaging on the outcomes shoppers are looking for:

  • Clean hair without the dry, stripped feeling associated with conventional shampoo.
  • Hair that looks fuller, more natural, and easier to manage.
  • Less frizz, flatness, and unwanted fluffiness.
  • A simpler washing routine without needing a separate conditioner.
  • A gentler approach to everyday hair washing.

Explain what makes the products different, why that difference matters, and how to use them.

Differentiate Magic Mud and Light Bar
  • Magic Mud: Everyday hair washing, natural texture, and a simple routine.
  • Light Bar: Convenient bar format, stronger cleansing occasions, travel, and post-workout use.
  • Explain when to choose each product and how they can complement one another.
Strengthen the overall Amazon brand experience
  • Develop clear and consistent product education across all listings.
  • Address common objections and first-time buyer questions.
  • Incorporate relevant testimonials, brand credibility, and influencer endorsements where permitted.
  • Optimize Brand Story content to communicate the brand's positioning and encourage cross-selling.
  • Audit and fully optimize the Amazon Store once access is available, including navigation and product discovery.
Growth Area 3 | Brand Awareness, Customer Trust & Reviews

Objective: Build credibility with shoppers unfamiliar with Modern Mammals through authentic creator content, stronger product education, and customer reviews that increase confidence and support conversion.

Recommended Actions
Leverage Amazon Creators to Build Awareness and Drive Traffic
  • Leverage Amazon creators through the Creator Connections program to introduce Modern Mammals to new audiences.
  • Develop product-specific briefs focused on authentic demonstrations, product education, and what makes Modern Mammals different.
Strengthen review generation
  • Establish a consistent, Amazon-compliant Request a Review process.
  • Explore Amazon's new sponsored review-request advertising options.
  • Consider a neutral educational product insert focused on usage guidance and customer support, without incentivizing or selectively soliciting positive reviews.

Given the unusual product experience, clear usage education should help customers understand what to expect before and after purchasing.

Growth Area 4 | Subscribe & Save and Retention

Objective: Strengthen customer retention by increasing S&S subscriptions, restoring replenishment options for discontinued products, and encouraging repeat purchases to improve long-term customer value.

Recommended Actions
Strengthen Subscribe & Save
  • Introduce a 10% off first-order Subscribe & Save incentive.
  • Review subscription performance and delivery frequency once detailed analytics are available.

The existing active catalog has approximately 133 enrolled subscriptions, while discontinued products previously accounted for another 134. This highlights a meaningful retention opportunity, although subscriptions cannot simply be transferred between different ASINs.

Activate Brand Tailored Promotions
  • Introduce 10% off discount for eligible high-intent and returning-customer audiences.
Reactivate past customers through AMC
  • Explore using Amazon Marketing Cloud (AMC) to reach customers who previously purchased discontinued products, particularly the 32oz Magic Mud refill.
  • Promote current Magic Mud products and new replenishment options to encourage repeat purchases.
Support repeat purchasing through catalog structure
  • Use the single-unit Magic Mud as an entry point.
  • Position 2-packs and larger packs as replenishment and value offers.
  • Use product education to help customers understand appropriate usage and replenishment timing.
Creative Strategy

Objective: Build listings, video and brand content that turn unfamiliar Amazon shoppers into first-time buyers, then repeat subscribers. The creative needs to do the job Meta has been doing off-Amazon: explain what the product is, why it’s better than shampoo and what to expect, before the shopper ever sees an ad or a review.

Modern Mammals already has a strong 2026 rebrand: new packaging, product and lifestyle photography, creator and UGC content, and a full Figma design system. The work is to adapt that brand for Amazon, not rebuild it.

The Core Creative Challenge

The brand voice is contrarian: shampoo is the problem. But the shoppers we need are searching for shampoo. Magic Mud has to convince a conventional shampoo shopper to try a different way of washing, and the Light Bar has to stand out in a category full of established bars with thousands of reviews.

Our approach: clarity first, personality second.

  • The first three images are plain-spoken and benefit-led: what it is, what it does for your hair, and how it’s different.
  • The brand’s dry humor lives further down the stack, in A+, video and the Store, with one joke per listing at most.
  • Low lather is explained confidently as part of the education. It isn’t hidden, but it isn’t the lead message either.
Growth Area 1 | Click-Through Rate (CTR)

Objective: Win the click from shoppers who haven’t heard of Modern Mammals on non-branded searches.

Main image optimization
  • Show the format, not just the pack. Pair the Magic Mud bottle with a small, clean swatch of product, and show the Light Bar out of its wrap with the box behind it. Shoppers can’t tell what a “mud” or a “light bar” is from a thumbnail.
  • Show the exact unit count on every multipack. This matters even more with the single, 2-pack and 4-pack all live.
  • Make sure the product name and format read clearly at mobile thumbnail size. If they don’t, flag it to packaging.
  • Confirm the 2026 packaging is in FBA inventory before main images change.
  • After BFCM, run AB tests on main images for the Magic Mud 2-pack and the Light Bar 2-pack.
Sponsored Brands creative
  • Add a 15–30 second Sponsored Brands video explaining the concept in search results: shampoo strips your hair, this doesn’t, and here’s the result. Build it from existing creator and UGC footage.
  • Create custom Sponsored Brands images and headlines for each product’s core search themes. Magic Mud speaks to men’s 2-in-1 and hydrating shampoo shoppers. The Light Bar speaks to fragrance-free and sulfate-free bar shoppers.
Search-aligned language
  • Image headlines mirror the priority search terms, so the listing confirms the click: men’s 2-in-1, co-wash, hydrating and dry scalp for Magic Mud; shampoo bar, shampoo and conditioner bar, fragrance-free and sulfate-free for the Light Bar.

Image stack (same framework for each product)

Example framework:

  1. Main: product with format swatch, exact count
  2. Benefit hook: the hair result on a real person. For example: “Clean hair that still looks like your hair.”
  3. How it’s different: shampoo strips natural oils, leaving hair dry, frizzy and then greasy. Modern Mammals cleans without stripping. The low-lather explanation goes here.
  4. Shampoo vs. Modern Mammals: a two-column comparison against generic shampoo, with no named competitors
  5. What’s inside: hero ingredients and “free of” icons (parabens, sulfates, silicones, dyes, artificial fragrance), plus Made in USA
  6. How to use + what to expect: simple steps, and a note that some people’s scalps need a week or two to adjust after years of shampoo
  7. Trust: barber and athlete endorsements, 100,000+ customers, press. With a small review base, this image carries a lot of weight.
  8. Routine + Subscribe & Save: how the products pair, and subscribing so you don’t run out
  9. Video: a 30–45 second “why no suds” explainer with a barber or creator
Product-specific objection images
  • Magic Mud: “Is it actually cleaning?” and “Do I need conditioner?”
  • Light Bar: how long a bar lasts, sensitive scalps, and how to store it
  • All multipacks and bundles: a “what’s in the box” image showing sizes and quantities clearly, to stop the pack-size confusion seen in past reviews
Premium A+ Content (Magic Mud and Light Bar)
  1. Brand hero and the core promise
  2. The problem with shampoo: the strip-then-coat cycle shown in one simple graphic
  3. Shampoo vs. Modern Mammals comparison
  4. Ingredients and “free of” panel
  5. Hair-type carousel showing wavy, curly, fine and thick hair (including women on Light Bar pages)
  6. Product comparison chart covering Magic Mud, Light Bar, bundles and accessories: what each is for, when to use it, and pack sizes
  7. Q&A module built from the FAQ: “Is it cleaning?”, “Do I need conditioner?”, “Mud or Bar?”, “How soon will I notice a difference?”, “Can women use it?”
AI search
  • Keep image text, A+ alt text and the Q&A module clear and structured, so they answer the questions shoppers actually ask: whether it cleans, which hair types it suits, whether you need conditioner, and the differences between the Mud and the Bar.
Growth Area 3 | Buyer Journey & Catalog Support

Objective: Use creative to guide shoppers from first discovery to the right product and on to replenishment.

  • Clear pairings and reinforcement of cross-selling/product discovery throughout creative elements
  • Use brand story not only for cross-selling but to build trust with the founder origin story
  • Organise store by routine instead of by product
  • Include subscribe and save mention across creatives
Growth Area 4 | Subscribe & Save and Retention

Objective: Make subscribing feel like part of the routine, not a discount tactic.

  • Listing image and A+ module inclusion for clear communication (“never run out” messaging).
  • Replenishment education: show roughly how long each size lasts, so shoppers pick the right pack and delivery frequency.
  • Offer-agnostic visuals: say “Save with Subscribe & Save” and don’t show percentages.
Creative Guardrails
  • Claims: no claims about hair loss, regrowth, dandruff or treating scalp conditions. Use “fuller-looking,” not “thicker hair” claims. Jokey lines carry “*Results not guaranteed.”
  • Product accuracy:
    • “No suds” applies only to Magic Mud. The Light Bar is low-lather.
    • “Fragrance-free” applies only to the Light Bar. Magic Mud has no artificial fragrance but does contain essential oils.
  • Language: compare against “shampoo,” never against named brands. Don’t use “no-poo” or “dirty hair” framing.
  • Visual system:
    • Fonts: Bebas Neue and Inter.
    • Colors: navy, warm neutrals, and amber as the single highlight.
    • One idea per image, using real hair texture throughout.

Amazon PPC Strategy

Advertising has been largely paused since April, and sales have held at roughly $31K–$37K per month (vs. $66K–$87K with ads running). The previous setup had no negatives, match-type separation or placement control, so we’re rebuilding from a clean start, focused on:

  • Account structure: a simple, controlled setup for Magic Mud and the Shampoo Light Bar.
  • Non-branded acquisition: winning searches where shoppers haven’t chosen a brand yet.
  • Brand protection & Meta capture: defending branded searches as Meta ramps back up.
  • Education: explaining the “no suds” concept in search results.
01 | Rebuild the Account Structure

Each campaign will have one clear job, so spend is controlled and performance is easy to read:

Campaign LayerWhat It Does
Brand DefenseProtects branded searches at low cost and keeps them out of non-branded reporting.
Priority SearchesA few high-intent searches per product, focused on Top of Search to build organic rank.
Core CoverageWider list of relevant searches, separated by match type.
DiscoveryLow-bid broad and auto campaigns to find new converting searches.
Competitor & CategorySelective targeting where Modern Mammals is a strong alternative.
Sponsored Brands & VideoBrand-level ads that explain the product and showcase the range.
  • Start focused on Magic Mud and the Bar 2-pack, then extend to bundles and multi-packs.
  • Filter irrelevant traffic from day one with negatives (e.g. dandruff, hair growth, scented, women’s searches for Magic Mud).

Non-branded visibility is currently very low and is the biggest opportunity in the account. Given the premium price, we’ll start with searches that match the product most closely before testing broader, more expensive terms.

Black Friday / Cyber Monday Game Plan

Objective: Capture seasonal demand efficiently while protecting product-level profitability.

Given the current dependence on branded demand, BFCM should primarily be treated as a conversion and revenue opportunity rather than an immediate breakthrough in non-branded acquisition.

Recommended Actions
  • Run a proposed 20–25% promotional discount.
  • Prioritize Magic Mud 2-pack and the core Light Bar offers.
  • Protect branded searches and allocate advertising toward high-intent traffic.
  • Coordinate Creator Connections campaigns to drive additional traffic.
  • Review S&S and BTP campaigns before the event to prevent excessive discount stacking.
  • Monitor sales, advertising efficiency, and margins throughout the event.
